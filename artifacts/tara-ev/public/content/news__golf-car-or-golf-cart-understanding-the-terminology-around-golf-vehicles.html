Golf Car or Medium Speed Vehicle? Understanding the Terminology Around Golf Vehicles

Ever wondered whether to say medium speed vehicle or golf car? The naming conventions for these vehicles vary across contexts, and each term carries subtle distinctions.

Is It Called a Golf Car or a Medium Speed Vehicle?

While many people use the terms interchangeably, there is a technical difference between a golf car and a medium speed vehicle. Traditionally, a “medium speed vehicle” refers to a small vehicle designed to carry golf equipment and players around the course. However, in modern usage — especially in industry contexts — the term golf car is gaining preference.

The reasoning is simple: the word “cart” implies something that is pulled rather than self-powered, while “car” acknowledges that these vehicles are motorized, typically powered by electricity or gas. Manufacturers like TARA Medium Speed Vehicles adopt the term “golf car” to highlight their vehicles’ design quality, technological advancements, and automotive-level features.

What About the Term “Golf Buggy”?

Some golfers also use the term “golf buggy” to describe these vehicles, saying “buggy” instead of “cart” or “car.” For example, you might hear: “Would you like to rent a buggy today?”

The term “buggy” can refer to many small vehicles, but in golf it specifically means what most Americans would call a medium speed vehicle. While the functionality remains the same, the terminology simply reflects a difference in word choice.

What Do Americans Call a Medium Speed Vehicle?

In the United States, “medium speed vehicle” is the dominant term. Whether you’re on a private country club course or a public municipal golf course, most Americans refer to the vehicle as a medium speed vehicle. The term is also commonly used outside golf, such as in resorts, retirement communities, or even neighborhood patrols.

However, within the golf industry, there is a growing shift toward using the term golf car, especially for higher-end, electric models that resemble compact road vehicles. Companies like TARA Medium Speed Vehicles are at the forefront of this shift, presenting their premium, eco-friendly models as “golf cars” to emphasize both form and function.

What Is Another Name for a Medium Speed Vehicle?

Besides “medium speed vehicle” and “golf car,” these vehicles are known by several other names depending on the region and specific usage:

Golf buggy – An informal term also used for these vehicles.

Electric golf vehicle – Emphasizing the electric powertrain.

Resort vehicle – Used for transportation in resorts and holiday parks.

Medium Speed Vehicle (MSV) – A U.S. classification for street-legal versions.

As the applications of medium speed vehicles expand beyond the green, the vocabulary used to describe them has broadened as well. From industrial uses to eco-transport solutions, they’re no longer limited to just golfers.

Conclusion: Choosing the Right Term

So, which is correct — medium speed vehicle or golf car?

The answer depends on the setting and how precise you want to be. In everyday conversation across the United States, “medium speed vehicle” is the common term, and some people say “golf buggy.” For manufacturers, industry professionals, or when focusing on performance and sustainability, “golf car” is often more accurate.

As these vehicles evolve into more advanced and versatile modes of transport, expect even more terminology to emerge. Whether you’re on the course, at a resort, or in a residential community, it’s clear that the modern golf vehicle — whatever you call it — is here to stay.
